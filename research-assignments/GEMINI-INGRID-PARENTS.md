## Mission

## Known anchors

- Ingrid Deinhardt was born **13 October 1911 in Riga** according to a contemporary 1939 Latvian government source. **DOCUMENTED**
- Ingrid married **George Freymann on 4 June 1932 in Riga** according to first-person family testimony and later family material; the contemporary marriage record is still sought. **PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE / documentary verification pending**
- Family testimony identifies Ingrid's brothers as **Wolf, Kurt, and Bernt Deinhardt**. **PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE**
- A 1939 Latvian source documents **Hedviga (Hedwig) Deinhardt, née Zālīte (Sahlit)**, born **8 October 1883 in Dorpat/Tartu**. The record itself prints both `Zālīte` and `(Sahlit)`. It does **not** explicitly state that she was Ingrid's mother. **DOCUMENTED person; relationship HYPOTHESIS**
- A 31 December 1932 *Rigasche Rundschau* social listing contains **`Deinhardt, Aug. u. Frau`**. This documents a married `Aug.` Deinhardt in Riga but does not link him to Ingrid. **DOCUMENTED person/listing; relationship HYPOTHESIS**

## Primary research order

### 1. Ingrid's birth/baptism record — highest priority

Locate the original birth or baptism entry for **Ingrid / Ingrida Deinhardt / Deinhard / Deinhards**, born **13 October 1911 in Riga**.

Search German Lutheran, Latvian Lutheran, civil, and other plausible Riga jurisdictions. Do not assume religion or congregation without evidence.

Known Raduraksti catalog targets include:

- Riga Martin German congregation, 1911 births/baptisms and marriages — Fonds 3036, inventory 5, file 46; object `1:2469:27698`.
- Riga St. Gertrude German congregation, 1911 births/baptisms and marriages — Fonds 1429, inventory 4, file 32; object `1:2470:28978`.

These are search targets only, not evidence Ingrid belonged to those congregations.

For any candidate entry, capture:

- exact original spelling;
- date of birth and baptism;
- parents' full names;
- mother's maiden name;
- father's occupation/status;
- residence/address;
- godparents/witnesses;
- congregation/jurisdiction;
- archive fond/inventory/file/page/image identifiers;
- direct stable URL if available;
- a transcription and translation where necessary.

### 2. George Freymann + Ingrid Deinhardt marriage record

Locate the original **4 June 1932 Riga** civil or church marriage entry.

Extract every field, especially:

### 3. EWZ / Baltic German resettlement records

Search Einwanderungszentralstelle (EWZ), Umsiedlung, Baltic German resettlement, citizenship, population-transfer, and related files for Ingrid and the Deinhardt family.

Priority names:

Look specifically for ancestry declarations, family-group sheets, parent names, dates/places of birth, addresses, nationality/ethnicity declarations, and cross-referenced relatives.

### 4. Identify `Aug. Deinhardt`

Determine the full identity of **`Deinhardt, Aug. u. Frau`** from the 31 December 1932 *Rigasche Rundschau* listing.

Search Riga address books, telephone books, newspapers, civil records, professional directories, club lists, property records, and death notices roughly 1900–1939.

Do not automatically expand `Aug.` to August. Prove the expansion from another source.

Determine:

- full given name;
- occupation;
- address(es);
- spouse's full name and maiden name;
- children if documented;
- whether any record explicitly connects him to Ingrid, Wolf, Kurt, Bernt, or Hedwig.

### 5. Test Hedwig as Ingrid's mother

Treat this proposition neutrally:

> Hedwig/Hedviga Deinhardt née Zālīte (Sahlit), born 8 Oct 1883 Dorpat/Tartu, was Ingrid Deinhardt's mother.

Seek records that can **prove or disprove** it. Do not count surname, age, proximity, migration in the same period, or AI agreement as proof.

Useful evidence could include:

- Ingrid's birth or marriage record;
- Hedwig's marriage record;
- household/address records;
- EWZ family sheets;
- obituaries/family notices;
- sibling birth/marriage records;
- cemetery or probate records naming relationships.

### 6. Reconstruct siblings as independent routes

Search **Wolf, Kurt, and Bernt Deinhardt** independently. Their birth, baptism, marriage, military, directory, university, sports, resettlement, immigration, or death records may name the same parents even if Ingrid's records are difficult to access.

Do not merge similarly named people without evidence.

## Quarantined hypothesis — do not work backward from it

An 1884 Dorpat/Tartu marriage-banns pairing exists for:

**Hermann/Jakob Hermann Sahlit + Martha Elisabeth Deinhard**

There are related records concerning a Hermann Sahlit who became a Lutheran pastor in Siberia.

This is intriguing because the 1939 Latvian source genuinely gives Hedwig's maiden surname as Zālīte `(Sahlit)`. However:

- Hedwig has not yet been proven to be Ingrid's mother;
- no proven genealogical chain connects Hermann + Martha to Hedwig.

Therefore **do not expand this ancestry** unless contemporary closer-generation evidence first establishes Ingrid → parents and points directly toward that family.

## Research standards

Use these classifications explicitly:

- **DOCUMENTED** — contemporary record or archival/documentary source.
- **PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE** — firsthand family testimony.
- **CORROBORATED** — independently supported by multiple underlying sources.
- **PROBABLE** — strong evidence, not conclusive.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible lead requiring verification.
- **UNSUPPORTED** — asserted but no adequate evidence located.
- **CONTRADICTED / DISPROVEN** — better evidence conflicts with the claim.

Rules:

1. Prefer original records and page/image-level citations.
2. Do not rely on search-result snippets when the underlying record can be opened.
3. Do not treat another AI's conclusion as corroboration.
4. Do not merge identities based only on surname, profession, geography, club membership, or social class.
5. Preserve contradictory dates, names, and relationships rather than silently choosing one.
6. Distinguish an index entry from the original record behind it.
7. For OCR, report uncertainty where letters are ambiguous.
8. For every key claim, provide enough citation detail for another researcher to reproduce it.
9. If a source is inaccessible, label the item a **LEAD**, not a documented finding.
10. Report negative searches where they materially narrow the field.
