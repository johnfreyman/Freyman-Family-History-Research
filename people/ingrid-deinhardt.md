# Ingrid Deinhardt Freymann

**Status:** Active reconstruction from the George & Ingrid Freymann family memoir and corroborating records.

## Identity

A 1939 Latvian government repatriation notice lists **Ingrida (Ingrid) Freimanis (Freymann), born Deinhards**, born **13 October 1911 in Riga**. The same notice places the family at **Zigfrīda Meierovica bulvāris 16, apartment 5, Riga**.

**Evidence:** DOCUMENTED contemporary government source + PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE.

## Established family relationships

George's memoir identifies **Wolf Deinhardt** as Ingrid's brother before their marriage. In SECOND HALF, Chapter 36, George says that after resettlement Ingrid was in Gotenhafen with her children and that **her brothers Kurt and Bernt** later moved to Gotenhafen and established an apartment of their own **with their mother in charge**. He separately says **Wolf and his family, with Karin and their two children, lived across the street**.

This establishes at least three brothers:

- **Wolf Deinhardt** — married **Karin** by the Gotenhafen period; at least two children.
- **Kurt Deinhardt** — living with his mother in Gotenhafen at the time described; exact marital status not established.
- **Bernt Deinhardt** — likewise living with his mother in Gotenhafen at that time.

The names of Ingrid's parents have not yet been securely established.

## Possible mother — Hedwig Deinhardt

The same 1939 Latvian repatriation material includes **Hedviga (Hedwig) Deinhardt, née Zālīte/Sahlit**, born **8 October 1883 in Tērbata/Dorpat (Tartu)**. She is the right generation and George explicitly says Ingrid's mother traveled with the family and later lived with Kurt and Bernt in Gotenhafen. However, the notice itself does **not** explicitly identify Hedwig as Ingrid's mother.

**Current status:** HYPOTHESIS — strong enough to pursue, not yet proven.

## Courtship and marriage

In FIRST HALF, Chapter 26 (“Bliss”), George says he met Ingrid through the **Riga Rowing Club**. Ingrid and her brother Wolf were champion dancers. Wolf became one of George's sailing companions and was a crew member on George's yacht *Roma I*; through Wolf, George's contact with the Deinhardt family deepened.

George says Ingrid did not initially enjoy sailing, but Wolf persuaded her to join some sailing parties. George and Ingrid both worked late office hours and often met afterward for supper. In autumn **1931**, after an evening at the Woermanns Park restaurant, George proposed while they were walking past his old school.

Their engagement was announced to both families at Christmas. George says neither had met the other's parents before the engagement announcement.

They married on **4 June 1932** in Riga. George describes a large wedding with a reception at the **Officers Club**, on whose premises the Deinhardt family had their home.

## Married life

George and Ingrid had four sons. Exact birth dates of later-generation family members are intentionally omitted from this public research mirror for privacy; the private master archive preserves them.

George says Ingrid furnished their Riga homes and that the family spent summers at the Freymann villa at Lielupe.

## 1939 resettlement and Gotenhafen family cluster

In SECOND HALF, Chapter 33, George says Ingrid, her children, his mother-in-law, a governess and others left Latvia aboard **Gneisenau** at the end of November 1939 during the Baltic German resettlement. George remained behind temporarily and followed on *Der Deutsche*.

In Chapter 36, George gives a useful picture of the Deinhardt family after resettlement: Ingrid's brothers Kurt and Bernt moved to Gotenhafen and set up an apartment with their mother; Wolf lived nearby with wife Karin and two children.

## Postwar lead — Bernt Deinhardt

A history of the **Baltischer Segler-Verband (BSV) Steinhude** records a **Bernt Deinhardt** as a committee member active in efforts to obtain club property at Steinhuder Meer in **1961–1962**. Given Ingrid's family sailing culture, the uncommon name, and the right generation, this is a strong lead for Ingrid's brother, but identity is not yet proven.

Source: https://wind.bsv-steinhude.de/verein/vereinshistorie/die-zeit-von-1952-bis-1963/

**Current status:** PROBABLE LEAD, not yet merged as documented identity.
