# Ingrid Deinhardt Freymann

**Status:** Active reconstruction from family memoir testimony and corroborating contemporary records.

## Identity

A 1939 Latvian government repatriation notice lists **Ingrida (Ingrid) Freimanis (Freymann), born Deinhards**, born **13 October 1911 in Riga**. The same notice places the family at **Zigfrīda Meierovica bulvāris 16, apartment 5, Riga**.

**Evidence:** DOCUMENTED contemporary government source + PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE.

## Established family relationships

Family memoir testimony identifies **Wolf Deinhardt** as Ingrid's brother before her marriage and later identifies **Kurt** and **Bernt Deinhardt** as additional brothers. The memoir also describes their mother living with Kurt and Bernt after Baltic German resettlement.

This establishes at least three brothers:

- **Wolf Deinhardt** — married **Karin** by the Gotenhafen period; at least two children.
- **Kurt Deinhardt** — living with his mother in Gotenhafen at the time described; exact marital status not established.
- **Bernt Deinhardt** — likewise living with his mother in Gotenhafen at that time.

The names of Ingrid's parents have **not** yet been securely established.

## Possible mother — Hedwig Deinhardt

The same 1939 Latvian repatriation material includes **Hedviga (Hedwig) Deinhardt, née Zālīte (Sahlit)**, born **8 October 1883 in Tērbata/Dorpat (Tartu)**. The contemporary source itself supplies both surname forms; `Sahlit` is not a modern research invention.

Hedwig is the right generation, and family testimony says Ingrid's mother traveled with the family during resettlement and later lived with Kurt and Bernt. However, the 1939 notice itself does **not** explicitly identify Hedwig as Ingrid's mother.

**Current status:** HYPOTHESIS — plausible and high-value, not proven.

## Possible father lead — `Aug. Deinhardt`, Riga 1932

A *Rigasche Rundschau* New Year supplement dated **31 December 1932**, headed `Neujahrs Visiten für das Jahr 1933`, includes the entry:

> `Deinhardt, Aug. u. Frau`

This documents a married `Aug.` Deinhardt in Riga only months after Ingrid's 4 June 1932 marriage. `Aug.` may stand for **August**, but that expansion has not yet been independently documented, and there is no contemporary source yet linking this man to Ingrid, her brothers, or Hedwig.

**Current status:** HYPOTHESIS / paternal candidate only.

## Courtship and marriage

Family memoir testimony says Ingrid met George Freymann through the **Riga Rowing Club** and through her brother Wolf. George and Ingrid married on **4 June 1932 in Riga**. The reception was held at the **Officers Club**, on whose premises the Deinhardt family was said to have their home.

The marriage date is therefore a major contemporary-record target because the civil or church entry may directly identify Ingrid's parents, residences, occupations, and witnesses.

## 1939 resettlement and Gotenhafen family cluster

Family memoir testimony says Ingrid, her children, her mother-in-law/maternal-family party and household members left Latvia aboard **Gneisenau** near the end of November 1939 during Baltic German resettlement. Later testimony places Kurt and Bernt living with their mother in Gotenhafen, while Wolf lived nearby with Karin and two children.

## Postwar lead — Bernt Deinhardt

A history of the **Baltischer Segler-Verband (BSV) Steinhude** records a **Bernt Deinhardt** as a committee member in **1961–1962**. The uncommon name, generation, and sailing context make this a useful lead, but identity has not been proven.

Source: https://wind.bsv-steinhude.de/verein/vereinshistorie/die-zeit-von-1952-bis-1963/

**Current status:** PROBABLE LEAD, not yet merged as documented identity.

## 1883–1884 Dorpat Sahlit–Deinhard theory — quarantined

An 1884 Dorpat/Tartu marriage-banns record pairs **Hermann/Jakob Hermann Sahlit** with **Martha Elisabeth Deinhard**. Other contemporary records concern a Hermann Sahlit who became a Lutheran pastor in Siberia.

Because Hedwig's 1939 Latvian record genuinely gives her maiden surname as Zālīte `(Sahlit)`, these records are potentially relevant. However, there is currently **no proven genealogical chain** connecting Hermann + Martha to Hedwig, nor a contemporary record proving Hedwig was Ingrid's mother.

**Current status:** HYPOTHESIS / documentary context only. Do not extend Ingrid's ancestry through this couple until Ingrid → parents is established independently.
