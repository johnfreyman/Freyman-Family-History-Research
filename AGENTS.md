# AI Research Protocol

This public repository is a curated research mirror of the private **Freyman / Freymann Family History** master archive. Multiple AI researchers may use it. Conversation memory and AI agreement are never authoritative; evidence is.

## Mission
Reconstruct the Freymann/Freyman and connected families rigorously from primary family sources, contemporary records, archival material, newspapers, maritime registers, and high-quality historical sources.

## Evidence labels
- **DOCUMENTED** — supported by a contemporary record or archival/documentary source.
- **PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE** — stated by George Freymann, Ingrid Deinhardt Freymann, or another firsthand family source.
- **CORROBORATED** — independently supported by multiple underlying sources.
- **PROBABLE** — strong evidence but not conclusively proved.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible research lead requiring verification.
- **DISPROVEN** — an earlier claim contradicted by better evidence.

AI agreement is not corroboration.

## Primary family source
A two-volume memoir by George and Ingrid Freymann is preserved in the private master archive. This public mirror contains findings extracted from it but intentionally does not expose the original family documents. If a claim depends on memoir text not reproduced here, label it **PRIMARY FAMILY SOURCE** and do not pretend to have independently verified the original passage.

## Mandatory research rules
1. Never convert inference into fact.
2. Do not silently reconcile contradictions.
3. Cite important claims with enough detail to relocate the underlying source: title, issuing body/author, date, page/chapter/record number when available, and stable URL.
4. Prefer original/contemporary sources over later compilations.
5. Separate source-derived facts from interpretation.
6. Preserve name variants: Freymann/Freimanis/Freiman/Freyman; Zalcmanis/Salcmans; Šnore/Schnore/Snore; etc.
7. Watch Julian vs Gregorian dates and historical place-name changes.
8. Do not use shared surnames, professions, geography, or social networks as proof of kinship.
9. Search snippets and AI summaries are leads, not final evidence when the underlying document is accessible.
10. When evidence conflicts with existing repo material, report the conflict explicitly rather than choosing whichever seems likelier.

## Research-quality hierarchy
1. Original vital/civil/church/immigration/court/company/property/military/government/ship-registry records.
2. Contemporary newspapers, directories, official notices, maritime registers, government publications.
3. Firsthand memoirs, letters, photographs with provenance, oral histories, family documents.
4. Scholarly histories, museums, archival finding aids, institutional databases.
5. Specialist maritime/genealogical databases identifying underlying sources.
6. Unsourced compilations, forums, derivative family trees and snippets — leads only.

## Current identity cautions
- Alice Brand Freymann appears in an external vessel history as co-owner of the first ship *Alice* with `M. Schnore`; George's memoir calls the partner Captain **Nikolai Schnore**. Do not merge them without proof.
- The Šnore/Schnore maritime family has documented Far East activity and marriage links to an Andersons maritime family. This does not prove that Oswald Andersons belongs to that branch.
- Hedwig/Hedviga Deinhardt née Sahlit/Zālīte is a plausible candidate for Ingrid's mother but must not be entered as fact without documentary proof.
- The first vessel *Alice* (ex-*Groningen*, acquired c. 1925/26, lost 1928) and wartime *Alice Freymann* (ex-*Venta*) are different ships.
- Reused wartime ship names require identification by build details/official number before records are merged.

## Handoff rule for outside AI researchers
Do not modify the private master. Return a structured handoff containing, for every meaningful finding: claim; person/entity; evidence label; exact source and URL; page/date/record number; concise evidence; reasoning; contradictions; confidence; unresolved questions; and recommended master-repo updates.

The goal is not the largest family tree. The goal is a defensible history where a future family member can tell what we know, how we know it, what remains uncertain, and exactly where the evidence came from.
